Warcrest · In-Game HUD · Landscape Study

The same seven hubs,turned on their side.

Every RTS this study borrows from — Warcraft III, StarCraft, Age of Empires, Command & Conquer — was designed landscape-first. Rotate Warcrest and the layouts don't just fit; most of them resolve into the shape they were always reaching for: shallow-and-wide consoles, full-height side rails, and clean left/right thumb zones. Here are all seven from the portrait study, re-laid for a landscape phone held in two hands.

Where I'd point the ship

Warcrest's own design doc names the target out loud: the Blizzard fantasy on a Thronefall control chassis, with the hero under your thumb. That sentence maps almost exactly onto a pairing — Grove Radial for commanding warbands with near-zero chrome, and the Sigil Wheel's hero cluster for the avatar's own kit. Build them as one system: field mostly bare, a radial that blooms on selection, and a persistent hero orb-cluster since the hero is always yours to drive.

If you'd rather stay legible to lapsed RTS players first, Iron Console is the safe, complete answer and the most direct fix for the current grey slab — ship it as the "classic HUD" option and offer the radial as the default. Every skin here already recolors through faction CSS variables, so whichever you pick carries the Crown, the Horde, and the Rimwalkers without a second layout.
